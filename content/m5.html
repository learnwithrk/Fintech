Objectives: understand FinTech regulation, RegTech, RBI sandbox, AML, KYC and tax issues.

5.1 FinTech Regulation Framework

Regulation balances innovation with consumer protection and stability. Indian regulators: RBI (banking, payments), SEBI (securities), IRDAI (insurance), PFRDA (pensions), IFSCA (GIFT City). Key laws: Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007, IT Act 2000, Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023.

5.2 RegTech and Ecosystem

RegTech uses technology to automate compliance: reporting, monitoring and risk management. Players: regulated firms, RegTech vendors, regulators (SupTech).

Example: Automated systems file suspicious-transaction reports without manual effort.

5.3 Financial Inclusion and Adoption

PM Jan Dhan Yojana opened over 50 crore accounts. Adoption barriers: low literacy, language, trust, and connectivity.

5.4 Role of the Central Bank and RBI Regulatory Sandbox

The RBI issues licences, supervises payment systems and protects customers. Its Regulatory Sandbox (2019) lets firms test innovations on a limited scale, with relaxed rules, in cohorts such as retail payments, cross-border payments and MSME lending.

5.5 AML, KYC and Tax

AML: under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002, entities must monitor and report suspicious transactions to FIU-IND. Stages of laundering: placement, layering, integration. KYC: identify customers (Aadhaar, PAN, video KYC) and re-verify periodically. Tax issues: GST on service fees, TDS on crypto (1%), 30% tax on virtual digital asset gains, and cross-border taxation of digital platforms.

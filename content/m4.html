Objectives: understand the technologies powering modern FinTech and open banking.

4.1 5G, Mobile Apps and Smartphones

5G offers ultra-low latency (about 1 ms) and high speed, enabling real-time trading, biometric authentication and IoT payments. Smartphones with biometrics and NFC are the primary banking channel.

4.2 Transforming Customer Experience

Personalised dashboards, chatbots, video KYC, voice banking and one-tap payments.

Example: HDFC Bank's EVA chatbot answers queries instantly.

4.3 Cloud Computing

On-demand computing with models IaaS, PaaS, SaaS. It offers elasticity and lower capex; RBI requires data localisation of payment data.

4.4 Web 2.0, Rapid Web Design and JavaScript

Web 2.0 introduced interactive, user-generated content. Rapid design uses responsive frameworks (Bootstrap) and prototyping tools. JavaScript (React, Node.js) powers dynamic banking dashboards and real-time updates.

4.5 AI and Machine Learning in FinTech

Applications: credit scoring, chatbots, algorithmic trading, underwriting. Fraud detection: ML models learn normal behaviour and flag anomalies.

Example: A card used in Chennai at 2 pm and in London at 2:10 pm is flagged and blocked automatically.

4.6 API-driven Banking

An API (Application Programming Interface) lets systems exchange data securely. Benefits: faster integration, new revenue streams, better customer experience. Trends: open banking, Banking-as-a-Service, embedded finance, Account Aggregator framework.

Objectives: define FinTech, trace its evolution, identify ecosystem players, and relate Kautilya's principles to modern finance.

1.1 Meaning and Definition

FinTech (Financial Technology) is the use of technology to design, deliver and improve financial products and services. The Financial Stability Board defines it as technology-enabled innovation in financial services that could result in new business models, applications, processes or products, with a material effect on financial markets and institutions.

Example: Paying a tea-stall vendor by scanning a UPI QR code replaces cash, a bank visit and a card machine with one smartphone action.

1.2 Characteristics

Digital-first delivery; customer-centric design; speed and low cost; data-driven decisions; scalability through cloud and APIs; 24×7 availability; and collaboration between start-ups, banks and regulators.

1.5 Types of FinTech

Payments (PhonePe), lending (Lendingkart), wealth management (Groww), InsurTech (PolicyBazaar), RegTech, PropTech, and cryptocurrency/blockchain services.

1.6 Merits and Challenges

Merits: financial inclusion, lower transaction costs, speed, transparency, personalisation. Challenges: cyber-security, data privacy, regulatory uncertainty, digital divide, and systemic risk from interconnected platforms.

1.7 Global Trends

Embedded finance, Buy-Now-Pay-Later, open banking, CBDCs, generative AI in customer service, and green FinTech (ESG data platforms).

1.8 Principles of Arthashastra in FinTech Applications

Objectives: explain digital wealth management, DeFi, big data, cryptocurrency and blockchain.

3.1 Wealth Management Process

Steps: client profiling → goal setting → asset allocation → portfolio construction → implementation → monitoring and rebalancing. Technology automates profiling, allocation and rebalancing.

3.2 Platforms, Interfaces and Advantages

Platforms such as Groww, Zerodha Coin and ET Money offer mutual funds and stocks through apps connected via APIs to exchanges, AMCs and depositories. Advantages: low cost, convenience, transparency, scalability and 24×7 access.

3.3 Robo Advisors (Case Study)

Robo advisors use algorithms to build and rebalance portfolios from a risk questionnaire. Examples: Betterment, Wealthfront, and in India Scripbox and Kuvera. Fees are typically 0.25–0.5% versus 1–2% for human advisors. Limits: weak handling of complex tax and emotional situations; SEBI's Investment Advisers Regulations apply.

3.4 Decentralized Finance (DeFi)

DeFi offers lending, borrowing and trading through smart contracts on public blockchains, without intermediaries.

Example: On Aave, a user deposits crypto as collateral and borrows against it automatically.
Risks: smart-contract bugs, volatility, no consumer protection.

3.5 Social Media Analytics and Big Data

Big data is described by the 5 Vs: volume, velocity, variety, veracity, value. Sentiment analysis of social posts helps predict market mood, assess credit risk and tailor products.

3.6 Cryptocurrency and Blockchain

Cryptocurrency is digital money secured by cryptography. Bitcoin (2009, Satoshi Nakamoto) was the first. Blockchain is a distributed, immutable ledger where blocks are linked by hashes and validated by consensus (Proof of Work, Proof of Stake).

Framework layers: network → consensus → data (blocks, transactions) → smart contracts → application. In India, crypto gains are taxed at 30% with 1% TDS and the RBI has not recognised crypto as legal tender.
